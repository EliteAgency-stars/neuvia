Neuropsychology studies the relationship between the structure of a nervous system and the cognitive processes it supports — memory, attention, language, executive control — and how those processes change when something goes wrong. It is a discipline of careful observation, structured testing and cautious inference.

**Synthetic neuropsychology** is the name NEUVIA gives to an analogous line of inquiry for artificial systems. It is our conceptual proposal, not an officially recognized clinical discipline.

## The question behind it

If humans need mechanisms to understand, supervise and regulate their own cognitive processes, why not develop computational equivalents for artificial intelligence?

Modern neural networks represent concepts, follow instructions, use tools and produce multi-step reasoning. They also fail in characteristic ways: they hallucinate, lose track of instructions in long contexts, become overconfident, repeat themselves, contradict earlier statements and drift over time. These failures are often silent.

## What the analogy borrows — and what it does not

The analogy borrows *method*, not *metaphysics*:

- **Structured assessment.** Neuropsychology relies on standardized tests that isolate specific functions. Synthetic neuropsychology aims for evaluation batteries that isolate specific computational functions: consistency, calibration, contextual stability, tool use.
- **Relating behavior to mechanism.** Where access is authorized, interpretability research can connect a behavioral failure to internal representations, much as neuropsychology relates symptoms to underlying systems.
- **Intervention and follow-up.** Assessment is useful when it informs a bounded intervention and a verification of its effect.

It does **not** borrow claims about inner experience. NEUVIA does not assume that AI systems have consciousness, emotions, suffering or mental illness. Terms such as "diagnosis" refer to computational analysis of measurable behavior.

## Why a separate frame helps

Several established fields already address parts of this problem: model evaluation, AI observability, mechanistic interpretability, uncertainty quantification and AI safety. Synthetic neuropsychology does not replace them. It proposes a unifying question — *how does this system's cognition fail, and how can another system help detect and correct it under authorization?* — and a dedicated layer whose only purpose is answering it.

## Open questions

- Which computational functions can be isolated reliably with black-box access alone?
- How should an evaluator's own errors be measured and bounded?
- When is an intervention safe enough to apply without a human in the loop — if ever?

These questions define NEUVIA's research agenda. We will publish methods and results — including negative ones — as the work progresses.
